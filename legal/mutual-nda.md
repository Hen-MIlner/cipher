## Before you use this draft

- Fill in every **[bracket]**.
- **Who is "Cipher" in this agreement?** A contract needs a legal person: a company, or an adult (for example a parent or guardian) acting for the project. Contracts with people under 18 can be hard to enforce or can be cancelled, so get advice, and have a parent or guardian sign too (see the consent block at the end).
- **Use it when** you share non-public information with a school, advisor, developer, designer, investor or potential partner.
- **Do not use it** to share students' personal information. That needs separate data protection terms (see the School Pilot Agreement).
- This draft is written in plain English on purpose. A solicitor may want to tighten the wording.

## Mutual Confidentiality Agreement

**Date:** [DATE]

**Parties**

1. **[CIPHER PARTY NAME]** of [ADDRESS] (**Cipher**)
2. **[OTHER PARTY NAME]** of [ADDRESS] (**the Other Party**)

Each is a **party**, and each may be a **Discloser** (the party sharing information) or a **Recipient** (the party receiving it).

### 1. Purpose

The parties want to share confidential information so they can [DESCRIBE PURPOSE, for example: "discuss a possible pilot of Cipher at the Other Party's school" or "discuss a possible working relationship on Cipher"] (the **Purpose**).

### 2. What is Confidential Information

**Confidential Information** means information that a Discloser shares with a Recipient in any form (spoken, written, electronic or otherwise) that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential. Examples: product plans, source code, designs, safety methods, pricing, business and growth plans, discussions with schools, and non-public data.

### 3. What is not Confidential Information

Information is not confidential if the Recipient can show that it:

1. is or becomes public without the Recipient breaking this agreement;
2. was already known to the Recipient, without a duty of confidence, before it was shared;
3. is independently developed by the Recipient without using the Discloser's information; or
4. is lawfully received from someone else who was free to share it.

### 4. What the Recipient must do

The Recipient must:

1. keep the Confidential Information secret;
2. use it only for the Purpose;
3. protect it with at least reasonable care, and no less care than it uses for its own confidential information;
4. share it only with people who need to know it for the Purpose (such as employees, advisers, parents or guardians, or contractors) and who are bound to keep it confidential; and
5. not copy it except as needed for the Purpose.

### 5. Disclosure required by law

If the law, a court or a regulator requires the Recipient to share Confidential Information, the Recipient must (where the law allows) tell the Discloser as soon as reasonably possible, and must share only what is required.

### 6. Personal information

The parties will **not** share personal information about students or other individuals under this agreement unless they first agree in writing on how it will be protected. Any personal information that is shared must be handled in line with the *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles, where they apply.

### 7. Ownership

The Discloser keeps all rights in its Confidential Information. Sharing it gives the Recipient no licence or ownership, except the right to use it for the Purpose.

### 8. No promise to continue

Neither party has to make a deal or continue talking. The Confidential Information is shared "as is", and the Discloser gives no promise that it is complete or accurate, unless a later written agreement says so.

### 9. How long this lasts

This agreement starts on the date above and lasts for [2] years, unless ended earlier by written notice. The duty to keep information confidential continues for [3] years after the agreement ends, and for as long as any trade secret stays secret.

### 10. Returning or deleting information

On written request, the Recipient must promptly return or delete the Discloser's Confidential Information and confirm that it has done so. The Recipient may keep copies that it must keep by law, or that are held in routine backups, and must keep protecting them under this agreement.

### 11. If something goes wrong

The Recipient must tell the Discloser promptly if it learns of any unauthorised use or sharing of Confidential Information. A breach may cause harm that money alone cannot fix, so the Discloser may ask a court for an order (such as an injunction) as well as other remedies.

### 12. General

1. **Whole agreement.** This is the whole agreement about confidentiality for the Purpose.
2. **Changes.** Changes must be in writing and agreed by both parties.
3. **If part is invalid.** If a part cannot be enforced, the rest still applies.
4. **No transfer.** A party may not transfer this agreement without the other's written consent.
5. **Signing.** This agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically.
6. **Notices.** Notices may be sent by email to the addresses below.
7. **Governing law.** The law of South Australia applies, and the parties submit to the courts of South Australia.
